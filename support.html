Crestline Support

Crestline names the mountains in your camera view and stays lined up with them.

Questions, a peak with the wrong name, or something not working? Email crestline.peakfinder@gmail.com. A screenshot helps, and with the lines off, one with diagnostics showing (long-press the status in the middle of the dock).

Common questions

Why are the labels a little off?

Phone compasses are often 5–15° off. Point at mountains with a clear skyline and hold still for a moment: Crestline matches the skyline it sees to the terrain and corrects itself. The status in the middle of the dock shows Compass in orange until then, and Locked in green once it has matched.

The lines still don't line up. What can I do?

Behind trees, roofs or a window there may be too little skyline to match. Tap the status to pause the view, then Line up, and drag the lines onto the mountains; turn them with two fingers to level them. Or drag a peak's label onto the real summit to pin it there.

Why isn't a peak I can see labeled?

Labels go to the most prominent peaks in sight (Fewer, Normal or More under Labels in Settings), and every other peak in sight gets a small dot you can tap. Pinch to zoom in and more labels fit. Peaks hidden behind a nearer ridge aren't shown.

Does Crestline work without a signal?

Yes, where you've used it before: it keeps the terrain, peaks and hikes it downloads. Before a trip, save a place in Explore › Offline areas. Anywhere else, a worldwide map built into the app gives a rougher view until better terrain can download.

A peak's name or height is wrong.

Crestline's peaks come from OpenStreetMap, which anyone can correct at openstreetmap.org. Or email us and we'll look into it.

Which iPhones does Crestline run on?

iPhones with iOS 17 or later.

Staying safe

Crestline names what you can see. It isn't a navigation or safety tool: plan with a proper map and current conditions.
