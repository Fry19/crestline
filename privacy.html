Privacy Policy

Crestline: Peak Finder · Last updated 12 November 2026

Crestline names the mountains in your camera view. It has no account, no ads and no tracking, and we don't collect your data. This page explains what the app uses, where it goes, and what stays on your iPhone.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Camera. Crestline looks at the camera picture on your iPhone to find the skyline. Pictures are never uploaded. A labeled picture is saved to your photo library only when you choose to save it.
  • Location. Your location is used on your iPhone to work out which mountains are around you. Crestline also keeps the heading it lines up at a place for up to a week, so it can start lined up when you come back. That stays on your iPhone.
  • Motion. The gyroscope, accelerometer and compass keep the labels on the mountains as you move. They're read on your iPhone and never sent anywhere.
  • Photos you pick. When you label a photo from your library, you choose it in Apple's photo picker, and Crestline sees only that photo. It reads where and which way the photo was taken, on your iPhone.
  • Downloads and settings. Terrain, peaks, hikes, imagery, offline areas and your settings are stored on your iPhone. Settings › Delete downloaded data removes the downloads; deleting the app removes everything.

What leaves your iPhone

To show the mountains around you, Crestline downloads data for that area from the services below. Each request names an area (a box or tile around where you are, or around a place you look at or save), not you. Like any internet request, it also carries your internet (IP) address and identifies the app as Crestline. These services are run by others, and their own privacy policies apply.

ServiceWhat Crestline gets from it
OpenStreetMap Overpass servers (overpass-api.de, openstreetmap.fr, kumi.systems, private.coffee, maps.mail.ru)Peaks, hikes and land cover
Copernicus terrain and USGS 3DEP, on Amazon Web ServicesTerrain heights
Wikidata, QLever (qlever.dev), Wikipedia and Wikimedia CommonsCrestline facts, summaries and photos
Apple MapsSatellite imagery, under Apple's privacy policy

If you share crash reports and analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may pass anonymous crash reports to us. Crestline itself sends no analytics.

Children

Crestline isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect anything from them; it doesn't collect personal data from anyone.
